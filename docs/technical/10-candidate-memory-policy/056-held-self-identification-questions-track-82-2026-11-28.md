**Held self-identification questions (Track 82, 2026-11-28).**
A protected answer answers only an identity question: the question a label asks, without its list hints, must be in full one of a fixed set of identity shapes — a bare category ("Gender", "Veteran Status", "LGBTQIA+ identity"), "What is your …", "Please select or identify your …", "How would you describe or identify your …", "What or which … do you identify as or with", "I identify my … as", "Are you / Do you identify as / Do you consider yourself" followed only by the category ("a protected veteran", "Hispanic/Latino", "transgender"), "Please indicate if you are …", "Do you have a disability?", Workday's "Please select the … which most accurately describes …", and the LGBTQ community consider-yourself question (`isIdentityQuestion`, rounds 2–3).
Only list-hint parentheticals such as "(mark all that apply)" are ignored; any other parenthetical stays part of the question.
Any other wording, such as a group, an advocate, an affiliation, customers, skills, interest, membership, or a noun after the category, is not an identity question; it stays a sensitive pause.
Hispanic, Latino, Latina, Latinx, Latine, and LGBT wordings are `eeo` questions (`classifyFieldRole`, packages/browser `src/ats/field-roles.ts`), so "Are you of Hispanic, Latinx or Spanish origin?", "Please indicate if you are Hispanic or Latino", and "LGBTQIA+ identity" are sensitive, are answered only from the saved answers, and never reach the AI pause review.
Plural "veterans" and "disabilities" wordings are `eeo` questions too.
A pronoun question is not `eeo`, even worded "gender pronouns": it takes the saved pronouns answer (the question mapper's `pronouns` key), a checkbox group's options included ("He/him/his" for a saved "he/him"), while "Gender identity and pronouns" still names gender and stays `eeo`.
Gender, race or ethnicity, and Hispanic/Latino lists whose label is at most 100 characters are filled without a decline option (Lever's U.S. EEO survey, INL's "Sex", Schonfeld's "Please identify your race", Workday's "Please indicate if you are Hispanic or Latino"); veteran, disability, sexual orientation, transgender, and LGBTQ+ lists still need one, and the race-only rule still needs exactly one decline option.
Lever's concatenated "I identify my ethnicity asSelect all that apply" counts as a "select all that apply" list, and "Bi-Racial" and "Mixed Race" options answer a saved "two or more races".
A combined option ("I am not a protected Veteran / I prefer not to answer") is answered by its part before " / ", so a saved "not a veteran" or "veteran, not protected" answer takes it.
An LGBTQ+ membership question ("LGBTQIA+ identity", "Do you consider yourself a member of the Lesbian, Gay, Bisexual and/or Transgender, Queer and Questioning (LGBTQ) community?") is answered from the saved sexual orientation and transgender answers together: Yes when either names an LGBTQ+ identity (gay, lesbian, bisexual, pansexual, queer, or transgender), No only for a saved heterosexual orientation with a saved not-transgender answer, and otherwise (asexual, one answer missing) it pauses.
The No direction assumes that a heterosexual, not-transgender candidate is not LGBTQ+; intersex and questioning are not covered by any saved answer.
"I do not identify as transgender" answers a saved not-transgender answer, and "I am a veteran or active military" answers any saved veteran answer, while "I am not a veteran or active military" stays held, since whether the candidate serves now is not saved.
An optional single veteran-classification checkbox ("Disabled Veteran", "Armed Forces Service Medal Veteran", "Recently Separated Veteran") or a newly separated veteran's discharge date is left empty for a saved "not a veteran" or "veteran, not protected" answer (`veteranClassificationLeftEmpty`, rule `veteran_classification_empty`), with a reason that never names the answer; a required one, any other saved answer, or none still pauses.
Veteran classification boxes and discharge dates are left empty only on their exact labels ("Disabled Veteran", "Newly Separated Veteran Discharge Date Month").
The stop re-check (`pnpm questions:recheck`) still skips sensitive pauses, so held runs pick these up through `pnpm pipeline:retry --held`, which plans them again.
The saved values still never reach a model, logs, notices, or reports; LGBTQ+ and "Latine" questions are never stored in question memory either.

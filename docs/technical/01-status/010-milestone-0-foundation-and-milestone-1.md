Sites with no adapter are walked deterministically toward their form (`packages/browser/src/site-navigation.ts`, `advanceToApplicationForm`, after the posting's own Apply entry when that reached no application field): the walker rejects a cookie banner, names a wall before counting fields, stops at the first page showing an application field (name, email, phone, resume, cover letter, transcript, work authorization, sponsorship, LinkedIn, or GitHub, never a search, job-alert, or newsletter box), and otherwise follows at most four entries, in this order: a guest choice ("Apply as guest", "Continue without an account"), the posting's Apply entry (`chooseApplyEntry`), "Apply manually", and, only on a page with no question, a plain Continue / Next / Start application; it never follows sign-in, account creation, third-party apply ("Apply with LinkedIn"), sharing, a cookie control, a link to another site, or a button that submits a form with inputs, and it never types, uploads, or submits.
A page that embeds a Greenhouse application is opened at the embed's address as an `embed` hop (`greenhouseEmbedTarget`, below); a clicked button that opens a new tab is followed only to the same site, the company domain, or a known ATS host; an entry that reaches a Workday posting ends the walk with the `adapter` stop, and the run continues as a Workday run.
The run saves the address the walk reached; continue and submit walk again when the reopened page shows no application field.
Cookie banners are rejected, never accepted (`packages/browser/src/cookie-consent.ts`, shared by the posting's Apply entry and the walker): a reject control first (OneTrust's Reject All, Decline), then necessary-only (or "Confirm My Choices" with every optional group off), then close only when no reject exists; never Accept all, Allow all, or a settings link, and a banner offering only accept gets no click.
Controls inside cookie-consent managers, share and copy-link boxes, honeypot text boxes ("Leave this field blank"), and Oracle Digital Assistant's chatbot controls on Oracle Cloud hosts (its required work-summary box, `#oda-work-summary-text-area`) are never fields.
Oracle Candidate Experience's "Import your profile from resume" box (`.apply-flow-block--profile-import`, captured 2026-11-27) is never a field: it parses a resume into the form and redraws it with new ids, so the resume goes only into the form's own upload (Track 63).
Oracle Cloud HCM's email step (`/apply/email`) is taken in start, continue, and submit (`packages/agent/src/oracle-email-step.ts`, driving `packages/browser/src/ats/oracle-apply.ts`, which is derived from Oracle's public Candidate Experience bundle and a read-only capture of the email step): before anything is typed the profile email must exist and equal the mailbox the agent reads (`INTERNSHIP_AGENT_GMAIL_ADDRESS`, with its app password); the saved email is typed and Next clicked once; the terms box is ticked only when the standing data-consent setting covers its statement, logged with the exact text ("Accepted data consent: '<statement>' (Oracle Cloud HCM email step at <host>)"); Oracle's default label "I agree with the terms and conditions" is accepted under the standing data-consent setting through the terms-of-use group (Track 8); without the setting the run stops at the terms step with nothing typed.
A tenant that asks for the email on the job page itself (the body's `data-route` "job-details.quick-email-verification", "What's your email?", captured from a run trace of 2026-11-27) is the same email step, and a Next outside the email box's form (the one visible Next or Continue button of the page's main region, outside a cookie notice) or one disabled until the email and terms are given is waited on for up to 3 s after typing, then clicked once (the markup is modelled, Track 63).
An application section page (`/apply/section/N`), whether the email step led there or the run opened it with a signed-in Oracle session, is read only once Oracle has drawn its form controls (outside the import box and the chatbot, the same count three reads in a row, up to 30 s), since Oracle draws its blocks after the page loads (`oracle.formReady`, Track 63).

Mail from the company's own domain counts as Oracle's too (Track 63): a sender whose registrable domain name is the posting company's name joined or the initials of three or more of its words, or that sits within the registrable domain of the posting's careers URL, both taken from the posting, never the mail; a single word of the name never counts (a national laboratory's code came as "STAFFING" at its own domain with the subject "Confirm your identity", headers read on 2026-11-27).
